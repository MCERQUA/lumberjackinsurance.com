24:T1bda,<p>Flagstaff is one of the best rental markets in Arizona, and it isn't close. You've got nearly 30,000 NAU students needing housing, a tourism stream headed for the Grand Canyon and Snowbowl, second-home owners in Munds Park and Kachina Village, and short-term-rental demand spilling over from Sedona. If you own a rental property up here, you're sitting on a strong asset.</p>
<p>But here's a mistake we see constantly: owners renting out a property while it's still on a regular homeowners policy. That's a gap that can cost you everything in a claim. Let's talk about why landlord insurance exists and why your Flagstaff rental needs it.</p>
<h2>Why a Homeowners Policy Won't Cut It</h2>
<p>A homeowners policy is written for an owner-occupied home — you living in the house you insure. The moment you rent that property to tenants, you've changed the risk in ways your homeowners carrier never agreed to cover.</p>
<p>If you file a claim on a homeowners policy for a property you're actually renting out, the carrier can <strong>deny the claim</strong> on the grounds that you misrepresented how the home was being used. After a fire or a major water loss, that's a catastrophe. You'd be paying to rebuild out of pocket while still owing on the mortgage.</p>
<p>What you need instead is a <strong>landlord policy</strong> — often built on a <strong>dwelling-fire (DP)</strong> form — designed specifically for non-owner-occupied rentals. It accounts for tenants, turnover, and the income side of owning a rental.</p>
<h2>What Landlord Insurance Covers</h2>
<p>A good landlord policy is built around the realities of being a property owner, not a resident. The core pieces:</p>
<h3>Dwelling and Other Structures</h3>
<p>This covers the building itself — the structure you're renting out — plus detached structures like a garage, shed, or fence. As with any property in Flagstaff, insure it to <strong>replacement cost</strong>, not market value, so you can actually rebuild after a fire or a winter snow-load collapse.</p>
<h3>Liability Protection</h3>
<p>As a landlord, you can be held responsible if a tenant or guest is injured on your property — an icy walkway in January, a loose stair rail, a slip on a wet entry. Landlord liability coverage helps with medical and legal costs from those claims. Given Flagstaff's long, icy winters, this protection earns its keep every year.</p>
<h3>Loss of Rents</h3>
<p>This is the coverage that makes landlord insurance different — and it's a big one. If a covered event (a fire, a burst pipe, serious storm damage) makes your unit uninhabitable, <strong>loss of rents</strong> reimburses the rental income you lose while the property is being repaired. For a Flagstaff owner counting on monthly rent to cover the mortgage, this can be the difference between a hiccup and a financial crisis.</p>
<h3>Optional Add-Ons Worth Considering</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Vandalism and malicious mischief</strong> — useful with high tenant turnover.</li>
<li><strong>Building ordinance or law</strong> — helps cover the cost of bringing an older building up to current code after a loss, which matters for many older near-campus rentals.</li>
<li><strong>Higher liability limits or an umbrella</strong> — smart if you own multiple units.</li>
</ul>
<h2>The Flagstaff &amp; NAU Student-Rental Market</h2>
<p>NAU's enrollment drives an enormous, reliable demand for off-campus housing. Owners renting houses and apartments around campus, in the neighborhoods off Milton, and throughout central Flagstaff stay busy year after year. It's a great business — with a few quirks landlord insurance is built to handle.</p>
<p>Student rentals tend to have:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>More occupants and more turnover,</strong> which raises the odds of accidental damage and liability claims.</li>
<li><strong>Heavy use</strong> on appliances, plumbing, and fixtures.</li>
<li><strong>Seasonal patterns</strong> tied to the academic calendar.</li>
</ul>
<p>A landlord policy priced for this kind of use protects both your building and your income through it.</p>
<h2>Short-Term Rentals: Airbnb Near Flagstaff and Sedona</h2>
<p>Short-term rentals are booming across Northern Arizona. Flagstaff is a basecamp for the Grand Canyon, Snowbowl, and Route 66, and nearby Sedona is one of the most in-demand vacation markets in the state. Munds Park, Williams, and Bellemont all see vacation-rental traffic too.</p>
<p>Here's the catch: <strong>a standard landlord policy may not fully cover short-term rental activity,</strong> and platform-provided coverage (like a host guarantee) often has real gaps and conditions. If you're running an Airbnb or VRBO, you need a policy that specifically contemplates short-term, transient occupancy. Otherwise you risk a denied claim after guest damage, a guest injury, or a fire between bookings.</p>
<p>We can help you match the policy to how you actually use the property — long-term lease, short-term rental, or a mix.</p>
<h2>Don't Forget: Vacancy and the School-Year Gap</h2>
<p>Flagstaff rentals have a rhythm. Student leases often empty out in May, and a unit can sit vacant for weeks or months before the fall semester. Vacant properties carry <strong>higher risk</strong> — undetected leaks, frozen pipes in a cold snap, vandalism — and many policies <strong>limit or exclude coverage</strong> once a property has been vacant beyond a set period (often 30 or 60 days).</p>
<p>If your rental will sit empty between school years or between guests, tell your agent. You may need a <strong>vacancy endorsement</strong> or a short-term adjustment to stay protected through the gap. Don't find out about a vacancy exclusion <em>after</em> a burst pipe floods an empty unit in January.</p>
<h2>Require Tenant Renters Insurance</h2>
<p>One of the smartest, cheapest moves a landlord can make: <strong>require your tenants to carry renters insurance</strong> and write it into the lease. Here's why it helps you:</p>
<ul>
<li>It covers the <strong>tenant's</strong> belongings, so they're not looking to you after a loss.</li>
<li>It provides <strong>tenant liability</strong> coverage, which can shield you when a tenant causes damage or injury.</li>
<li>It signals responsible tenants and reduces friction after a claim.</li>
</ul>
<p>Renters insurance costs a tenant very little — often well under $20 a month — so it's a reasonable, common requirement. We can even help your tenants get covered.</p>
<h2>Protect Your Flagstaff Investment</h2>
<p>Owning a rental in Flagstaff is a smart play in one of Arizona's strongest markets — but only if it's insured the right way. A landlord/dwelling-fire policy with loss of rents, proper liability, and the right vacancy and short-term-rental provisions is what turns a property into a protected investment.</p>
